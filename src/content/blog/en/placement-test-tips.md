A placement test is not a pass-or-fail exam. Its purpose is to identify the class where you can learn effectively—not feel lost and not repeat material you already know. A calm, honest attempt gives the teaching team the clearest picture of your current English.

## 1. Arrive rested and unhurried

Sleep well, allow enough travel time, and bring anything the admissions team requested. Rushing can affect concentration more than a forgotten grammar rule.

## 2. Review, but do not cram

A short review of familiar vocabulary, common verb forms, and everyday reading is useful. Avoid memorizing advanced material at the last minute; the test needs to reflect the English you can use independently.

## 3. Read every instruction carefully

Placement questions may test grammar, vocabulary, reading, listening, or writing in different ways. Take a moment to understand what each task asks before answering.

## 4. Do not guess your way into a higher level

An artificially high result can place you in a class that moves too quickly. If you are unsure, make your best genuine attempt and let the full set of answers show your current strengths and gaps.

## 5. Be ready to speak naturally

If the assessment includes conversation, focus on communicating clearly. Use the vocabulary you know, ask for clarification when needed, and do not worry about having a perfect accent.

## What happens next?

The result helps the team recommend the most suitable starting point and course pathway. Ask admissions about the result, the recommended level, the schedule, and what progress looks like before you register.
